The Inflight Discord Bot

Setup guide for crew center owners

About 10 minutes, start to finish. You need to own the crew center and have Manage Server in your Discord.

The short version

  1. In your crew center: Settings → Alerts → Discord bot. Press Add the bot to your server, then Make a link code.
  2. In Discord, run /crew-admin setup code:XXXX-XXXX with that code.
  3. Run /crew-admin settings and pick at least a staff role and a ticket channel.
  4. In your recruitment channel, run /crew-admin panel.
  5. Run /crew-admin check and fix anything it marks ❌.

The crew center's setup guide has a "Discord bot" step that ticks itself off once all five are done.

What the bot does

One Inflight bot serves every crew center. Once it is linked to yours, it runs your recruitment and your airline's everyday questions from inside your own server:

  • Recruitment tickets. An Apply button opens a private thread for the applicant and your staff. The application is filled in there — or, for somebody who already applied on your website, linked with their application code — and lands in your crew center exactly as if it came through your join page: same requirements, same callsign checks, same Infinite Flight verification.
  • Entrance tests. When your crew center has an entrance test (Recruitment → Joining), it is posted in the ticket by itself. The score comes back to the same ticket the moment it is submitted. Staff can resend it, or send one anyway when you don't require it.
  • Crew center logins. Accepting — by staff, or automatically on a pass — creates the pilot's login and gives them your pilot role. The pilot opens a Choose my password link from a button only they can see; nothing is ever posted in the thread.
  • Help tickets for anything. A Contact staff button (or /crew ticket) asks what it's about, then opens a private thread with your staff — for a question, a problem, a leave request, anything. Tickets can be closed with a reason and reopened.
  • A warm welcome. Newcomers are greeted in your welcome channel with your own message and Apply / Contact staff buttons — and when you send website applicants to Discord, they're greeted where tickets open even before you set a welcome channel. A pilot who rejoins gets their pilot role back automatically.
  • Inactivity, handled kindly. Pilots who stop flying move to an inactive role and are told — nicely — how to stay. The moment they fly again their pilot role comes back. Removal after a month is optional and always comes with a reminder first.
  • Discord linked to the crew center. Pilots accepted through a ticket are linked automatically. Everyone else runs /crew link — one button, and they can sign in with Discord from then on.
  • Your airline on demand. /crew links, /crew stats, /crew events and /crew pilot answer from your crew center.
  • Pilot commands. /crew me (hours, rank, hours to the next rank, streak, this month's position), /crew leaderboard and /crew route (a route to fly, filtered by airport or aircraft).
  • Event sign-up in Discord. I'm in and Can't make it on every event post, a sign-up menu on /crew events, and a ping to everyone going an hour before.
  • Events with threads. Every event you publish gets a post in your events channel with its own discussion thread, kept up to date when you edit or cancel it.

The bot does not replace your Discord alerts (the webhooks in the same Alerts tab). Those keep posting applications, flight reports and route changes as before.

Before you start

  • You are the owner of the crew center (or a staff member with the "Connect Infinite Flight & the data store" permission). Only they see the Discord bot card.
  • Your crew center's database is connected. Applications are stored in it.
  • You have Manage Server in the Discord server you want to use.
  • Optional: an entrance test built under Recruitment → Quizzes and picked under Recruitment → Joining. Every applicant is sent it automatically.
  • Optional: under Recruitment → Joining, tick Send website applicants to our Discord (and set your Discord invite) to have people who apply on your website come to your server and open a ticket.
  • Optional: a role for your staff and a role for your pilots. You can create them now or use ones you already have.

1. Add the bot

  1. Open your crew center dashboard and go to Settings → Alerts. Scroll to Discord bot.
  2. Press Add the bot to your server. Discord asks which server — pick yours and approve the permissions it lists. (Why each one is needed is in Permissions.)
Inflight's own server cannot be linked. The bot already works there for Inflight; link your airline's server instead.

2. Link your server

Linking takes two halves on purpose: a code from the crew center proves you run the airline, and running the command in Discord proves you run the server. Neither alone links anything.

  1. Back on the Discord bot card, press Make a link code. You get something like ABCD-2345.
  2. In any channel of your server, type /crew-admin setup, paste the code into code, and send.
  3. The bot replies (only you can see it) with ✅ Linked to your airline.
  • A code works once and lasts 15 minutes. Making a new one cancels the old one.
  • Capitals, spaces and the dash don't matter when you type it.
  • A server can be linked to one crew center at a time. To move it, run /crew-admin unlink first.

3. Choose roles and channels

Run /crew-admin settings and fill in any of these. You can run it again later to change one thing — options you leave out keep their current value. Running it with nothing shows what is set.

OptionWhat it doesNeeded?
staff_roleWho handles tickets: sends tests, accepts, declines, closes. Anyone with Manage Server can too.Yes
ticket_channelThe text channel ticket threads open under. Make it one regular members can't post in — the threads themselves are private.Yes
pilot_roleGiven to a pilot when they are accepted.Recommended
log_channelA staff-only channel where the bot notes every ticket opened, accepted, declined and closed.Recommended
events_channelWhere published events are posted, each with its own thread.Optional

4. Permissions

The invite asks for exactly what the bot uses — never Administrator.

PermissionUsed for
View Channels, Send Messages, Embed Links, Read Message HistoryPosting the panel, tickets, results and events.
Create Private Threads, Send Messages in Threads, Manage ThreadsOpening, locking and archiving ticket threads.
Create Public ThreadsThe discussion thread under each event.
Manage RolesGiving the pilot role on acceptance, the welcome role on joining, and swapping the pilot and inactive roles.
Kick MembersOnly if you turn on removal with kick_after_days. At most ten people per sweep, each sent a kind goodbye first. Servers that added the bot before this was asked for: grant it to the bot's role, or re-add the bot from the Discord bot card.
Mention @everyone, @here and All RolesPinging your staff role in a ticket when it isn't set to "anyone can mention". Every message the bot sends is restricted to the one role and the one applicant it means — it never pings @everyone.

Three things Discord needs from you

  • Role order. In Server Settings → Roles, drag the bot's role above your pilot, welcome and inactive roles. Discord won't let a bot give out a role ranked above its own.
  • Staff can see every ticket. Give your staff role Manage Threads in the ticket channel. Without it, staff only see the tickets they're pinged into.
  • Channel overrides. If your ticket or events channel is locked down, make sure the bot's role is allowed in it.

Then run /crew-admin check. It goes through each channel and role and says in plain words what is missing — for example "Pilot role: drag my role above it in Server Settings → Roles".

5. Post the panel

Go to the channel recruits will see first and run /crew-admin panel (or pick a channel with the channel option). The panel shows your airline's name, tagline, banner and join requirements, with four buttons:

  • Apply — opens an application ticket.
  • Contact staff — opens a support ticket.
  • Links — your airline's links, only to the person who pressed it.
  • Crew center — your sign-in page.

If you haven't set a ticket channel, tickets open under the channel the panel is in. Post the panel again whenever you like; old panels keep working.

For a help-only panel (say in #support), run /crew-admin panel type:Help. It has Open a ticket, Link my account and Crew center.

Welcome

Run /crew-admin welcome with any of:

  • channel — where newcomers are greeted, with Apply, Contact staff, Already a pilot? Link and Crew center buttons.
  • role — a role everybody gets on joining (for example Visitor).
  • message — your own words. Use {user} (mentions them), {name}, {server}, {airline} and {members}. Type \n for a new line.
  • dm — also send the welcome by direct message.
  • test:True — posts a sample for you right now. off:True turns it off.

Somebody whose Discord is already linked to a pilot on your roster is welcomed back instead, by callsign, and gets the pilot role straight away. If lots of people join within a minute (a raid, or a big import), roles are still given but the greetings pause until it calms down.

Help tickets

Tickets aren't only for joining. Contact staff, Open a ticket or /crew ticket asks two quick questions — what it's about, and any details — then opens a private thread only that member and your staff can see, pinging your staff role with the summary.

  • Close — the Close ticket button, or /crew close reason: inside the thread to give a reason. The member is told kindly (by DM if staff closed it) and the thread is locked.
  • Reopen — the closed message has a Reopen button for the member or staff.
  • Add someone — staff run /crew add member: in the thread.
  • Pilots in the inactive role get a Request leave button that opens a ticket titled Leave of absence.

Inactivity

Run /crew-admin inactivity role:@Inactive channel:#inactive. Optional: after_days (default 30, how long without a flight) and kick_after_days (default 0 = never remove).

  1. Every few hours the bot checks your crew center's approved flight reports for each pilot whose Discord is linked.
  2. A pilot with no flight in after_days gets the inactive role (and loses the pilot role), with a friendly note in your inactive channel: fly any route to stay, or press Request leave. No channel set? It's sent by DM.
  3. As soon as they fly and the report is approved, the roles swap back and they get a "welcome back" message.
  4. With kick_after_days set, a reminder goes out a week before. If they still haven't flown, they get a kind goodbye (with your Discord invite, if you've saved one) and are removed. Their logbook stays in the crew center.

The note to an inactive pilot has a Suggest a route button, so getting back in the air is one press away.

Never touched: pilots on leave of absence, staff, and anybody who hasn't linked Discord (the bot can't know who they are). Run /crew-admin sweep first — it shows exactly who would be moved, reminded, removed or welcomed back without changing anything. /crew-admin sweep apply:True runs it straight away.

Safety limits: at most 25 people are moved and 10 removed in one sweep; if the crew center can't be read completely, the sweep does nothing. Turning removal on never removes anyone sooner than eight days from that moment.

Linking Discord to the crew center

  • Joined through a ticket? Already done — accepting links their Discord to the login it creates, so Sign in with Discord works the first time.
  • Everyone else: /crew link (or the Link buttons on the welcome and help panel). It opens their pilot page, signs them in if needed, and goes straight to Discord's Authorize screen. Their pilot role is given the moment it's linked.
  • Already linked? /crew link says which pilot they're linked to.

How recruitment works

It's the same road as your website, set in one place — Recruitment → Joining in the crew center: Apply → (entrance test) → accepted → they choose a password. Whether accepting is done by staff or automatically, and which test, are set there; the bot follows.

What the applicant does

  1. Presses Apply. A private thread opens that only they and your staff can see.
  2. Already applied on your website? They press I applied on the website and enter the application code from their email or status page (it looks like ABCD-1234; pasting their whole status link works too). The application is linked to the ticket — they don't fill anything in again.
  3. New? They press Start application and fill in their Infinite Flight Community name, the callsign number they'd like and, if they want emails, their email address. If your airline has more than one callsign, they pick which. Then your join form questions, up to five per screen, and Submit — or I agree & submit if you have agreements.
  4. If you have an entrance test, its Take the test link is posted in the ticket straight away. No account is needed to sit it.

If the crew center refuses the application — requirements not met, callsign taken, IFC name not found — they see the exact reason with a button to fix it. Nothing is sent until it passes.

What your staff do

The ticket shows the application summary with these buttons. Your staff role is pinged when there's something to decide — no test, or a pass that's waiting for you — not while the applicant is still taking the test:

ButtonWhat happens
Send testRe-posts their test link, or sends one when you don't require a test. With more than one test built, staff pick which. The result is posted back to the ticket automatically.
AcceptAccepts the application — with or without the test — adds them to your roster, creates their crew center login, links their Discord to that login and gives the pilot role (and takes away the welcome role). The applicant gets a Set up my login button.
DeclineAsks for an optional message to the applicant, then tells them in the ticket.
CloseLocks and archives the thread. The applicant can close their own ticket too.

Their Discord is linked the moment they're accepted

Whether they're accepted from the ticket, automatically on a pass (when your crew center accepts automatically), or from Roster → Applications in the crew center, the ticket follows through: their Discord is linked to the new login, they get the pilot role, and the welcome with Set up my login appears in the ticket. If the link can't be made for them (for example, that Discord is already linked to another login), the welcome gives them a Link my account button and your log channel says why.

Everything staff do in Discord is the same action as in the crew center, and where the crew center notes who did something (who sent a test, who made a login) it shows the staff member's Discord name. You can also finish any application from Roster → Applications — the ticket still gets the test result.

The login

  • Set up my login answers only the applicant, with a reply nobody else can see: their username and a Choose my password button.
  • The link works once. Nobody — your staff included — ever sees their password.
  • An unused link expires after 14 days. To make a new one, use New link on their card under Roster → Applications → Invited in the crew center.

Open joining

If your crew center accepts automatically, a submitted application — or a pass, when you have a test — puts the pilot on the roster with their login straight away, and the welcome with Set up my login appears in the ticket.

Limits that keep things tidy

  • One open application ticket and one support ticket per person. Pressing Apply again links to the one they have.
  • 20 seconds between opening tickets, and at most 250 open tickets per server.
  • If two people press the same button at the same moment, the second is told "Still working on the last press — one moment" instead of doing it twice.

Events

With an events_channel set, every event you publish in the crew center is posted there with its time (shown in each reader's own timezone), route, aircraft, server and banner, and a thread is started under it for discussion.

  • Edited: the post updates and the thread gets a note.
  • Cancelled or removed: the post is marked and the thread is locked.
  • Drafts are never posted.

Signing up from Discord

  • Every event post has I'm in and Can't make it. Pilots sign up as themselves, with the same rules as the crew center: rank locks, slots and the waitlist. The post shows how many are going.
  • /crew events has a Sign me up for… menu.
  • An hour before an event starts, everyone going is pinged in its thread.
  • Signing up needs a linked Discord. Anyone who isn't linked yet is given the Link my account button.

If your Events webhook alert points at the same channel, you'll see both. Point one of them somewhere else, or clear the webhook.

Troubleshooting

You seeWhat to do
The /crew commands don't appearDiscord can take a minute after the bot joins. Restart Discord (Ctrl/Cmd + R). If they still don't show, re-add the bot from the Discord bot card — the invite includes the commands permission.
"This server is not linked to a crew center yet."Run /crew-admin setup with a fresh code (step 2).
"That code is wrong or has expired."Codes last 15 minutes and work once. Make a new one on the Discord bot card.
"This server is already linked to another crew center."Run /crew-admin unlink, then /crew-admin setup again. Your code isn't used up by the refusal.
"Tickets are not set up here."Set a ticket_channel that is an ordinary text channel (not a forum or announcement channel).
"I could not open a private thread."The bot needs Create Private Threads, Send Messages in Threads and Manage Threads in the ticket channel. Run /crew-admin check.
Staff can't see a ticketGive the staff role Manage Threads in the ticket channel (Permissions).
"I could not give them the pilot role"Drag the bot's role above the pilot role in Server Settings → Roles. The pilot is still accepted.
"This airline has no entrance test ready."Build one under Recruitment → Quizzes in the crew center, with at least one question.
"We couldn’t find an application with that code."The code is on the applicant's status page and in their "application received" email. A declined application can't be linked; they can press Start application to apply again.
"That application is already linked to a different Discord account."Each application belongs to one Discord account. If it's genuinely theirs, accept them from Roster → Applications and have them run /crew link.
"Only staff can …"The person pressing needs your staff role or Manage Server. Check staff_role in /crew-admin settings.
"There is no login waiting for you right now"Their link expired or was thrown away. Make a new one with New link under Roster → Applications → Invited.
"The crew center is busy right now." or "Still working on the last press"Many requests at once. Wait a few seconds and press again — nothing was done twice.
A pilot wasn't moved to inactiveTheir Discord isn't linked (ask them to run /crew link), they're on leave or staff, or their latest flight report is still waiting to be approved.
"I could not remove …"Give the bot Kick Members and drag its role above theirs — or set kick_after_days:0.
"Already accepted."Someone (or an automatic acceptance) got there first. The welcome with Set up my login is already in the ticket.

Still stuck? Open a ticket in the Inflight Discord and include what /crew-admin check says.

What is stored, and unlinking

  • Inflight keeps: which server is linked to your crew center, its name, who linked it, the role, channel and welcome choices above, which ticket thread belongs to which application, and — for inactivity — who is in the inactive role and when they'd be reminded or removed.
  • Discord links live on the pilot's login in your own database, like the Link button on their pilot page. Pilots can unlink from that page any time.
  • While an application is being filled in, its answers are held so the applicant can go back and edit. They are cleared the moment it is submitted — from then on the application lives in your own database, like every other.
  • Nothing to sign in with is ever posted. The choose-your-password link is read from your crew center each time the applicant presses Set up my login, shown only to them, and stops working once they've used it.
  • To unlink: press Unlink next to the server on the Discord bot card, or run /crew-admin unlink. Open tickets stay where they are; nothing new is posted. Removing the bot from your server unlinks it too.
