Your positions


Tap a position to turn it on or off. Work one, two or all three. Approach vectors arrivals to the ILS and departures out. Tower runs the runways: landings, takeoffs, line up and wait, crossings. Ground handles pushback and taxi on the real taxiways. The system works whatever you don't, and hands aircraft to you when they reach your frequency.

Terrain

On: minimum vectoring altitudes come from the real terrain, sector by sector. Below one sets off a low altitude alert; below the ground, you lose the aircraft. Off: one safe altitude everywhere. The map shows the land either way.

Weather

On: storm cells drift across the scope and pilots ask to deviate around them. Low ceilings and gusts in the ATIS, and below ILS minimums an aircraft goes around at decision height.

Emergencies

Rare: now and then (a few flights in a hundred) a pilot declares a mayday or pan-pan: an engine failure, a medical, low fuel. Give it priority, get it on the ground, and the runway closes for two minutes while the fire crews check it.

Arrivals Blue targets check in from Center. Descend them, vector them to join the localizer at 30° or less, at least 2 miles outside the approach gate (8 miles out), and below the glideslope. Then: l190 a30 i22L — "turn left heading 190, maintain 3,000 until established on the localizer, cleared ILS runway 22L approach."
Landing When they call the tower on final, clear them to land (cl). No landing clearance by short final and they go around. Only one aircraft on a runway at a time.
Departures Gold strips hold short. Line them up (luaw) or clear them for takeoff (to, with a heading if you want one), then climb them to the exit altitude and send them out through their gate. Wait 2 minutes behind a heavy, 3 behind a super.
Separation 3 miles or 1,000 feet. More in trail behind heavies, B757s and supers (4–7 miles). 2.5 miles in trail inside 10 miles on the same final; 1.5 miles diagonal on dependent parallel approaches; none needed between independent parallels 4,300 ft apart.
Speed 250 knots max below 10,000 feet. Minimum 210 knots, or 170 within 20 miles of the field. No speed changes inside the final approach fix. An approach clearance cancels your assigned speed unless you restate it.
Commands h270 l090 r180 heading · a50 fl190 altitude · s210 speed · i22L ILS · cl land · dct FIX · hold FIX · l360 · luaw · to · ga. Or drag from a target to point it at a heading.
Keys Space pause · Tab next aircraft · ←/→ heading · ↑/↓ altitude · Enter transmit · / command line · Esc deselect.
The rulebook The separation, approach, speed and runway rules come from FAA JO 7110.65, the controllers' handbook. Breaking one costs points, and the log names the rule.
